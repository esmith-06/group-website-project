Website Audit

For this assignment, we worked as a group to complete a website audit of Cedar Pointe Golf Course. Our goal was to evaluate the business's website and identify opportunities to improve its design, customer experience, search engine visibility, and overall performance. We selected four pages to evaluate throughout the project: the homepage, Course Information, Latest News, and Outings & Weddings.

We divided our audit into four main sections: user interface (UI), user experience (UX), on-site SEO, and website performance.

User Interface (UI):

We evaluated the website's overall appearance, including its colors, fonts, images, buttons, spacing, and consistency across pages. We identified areas for improvement, such as making the "Book A Tee Time" button more noticeable, breaking up lengthy paragraphs, adding more content to the Latest News page, and including additional event photos and customer testimonials on the Outings & Weddings page. We also used Chrome Developer Tools to measure color contrast and identify accessibility issues.

User Experience (UX):

We evaluated how easily visitors could navigate the website and find important information. We completed the WWWWWW test to determine whether the homepage clearly communicates what the business offers, why customers should choose it, and what action they should take next. We also created three realistic customer tasks involving tee time pricing, season passes, and event information. Each group member independently completed the tasks and recorded completion times, clicks, and areas of confusion. All members successfully completed the tasks, but we identified opportunities to make certain information easier to find.

On-Site SEO:

We researched 12 potential keywords and selected five target keywords to evaluate Cedar Pointe's search visibility. We checked its Google rankings and examined the four pages for keyword placement in URLs, title tags, headings, main content, image alt text, and anchor text. We also evaluated meta descriptions, word counts, canonical tags, and indexed pages. We identified opportunities to improve image descriptions, correct spelling errors, and add missing meta descriptions.

Website Performance:

We used Chrome Developer Tools and Lighthouse to evaluate all four pages on desktop and mobile devices. We recorded performance scores, loading times, accessibility, and other technical measurements. We found that the website performed noticeably better on desktop than mobile, with mobile performance scores ranging from 62–65. We also identified slow loading times, large image files, and opportunities to improve caching and image optimization.

This assignment allowed us to apply concepts from class to a real business website. We used research, testing, and measurable results to identify what Cedar Pointe is doing well and what could be improved to make its website more effective for potential customers.
